Post-Carotid Stenting Surveillance

Follow-up imaging, duplex velocity criteria for in-stent restenosis, and when to re-intervene after CAS

01 · The Problem

The stent is placed — the risk is not over

CAS treats the lesion of the day. Restenosis, plaque progression and recurrent embolic events develop silently over the following months and years. Structured surveillance is how we catch them before the next stroke.

~5–6%significant in-stent restenosis (≥70%) within the first 1–2 years
12–24 mowindow when neointimal hyperplasia — the dominant mechanism — peaks
>50%of restenoses are asymptomatic — imaging, not symptoms, finds them
Why it matters Most ISR is clinically silent until it embolises or occludes. Surveillance converts an unpredictable late event into a scheduled, treatable finding.

What are we watching for?

The targets of surveillance after CAS

01 · Targets

Six things to look for at follow-up

In-stent restenosis

Neointimal hyperplasia → recurrent narrowing. The #1 surveillance target.

Recurrent ipsilateral stroke / TIA

Embolic or flow-limiting. Symptoms always override imaging.

Contralateral progression

Atherosclerosis is bilateral — the other side can advance silently.

Hyperperfusion syndrome

Early (hours–days): headache, seizures, ICH after flow restoration.

Stent integrity

Fracture, deformation, incomplete apposition — rare but late risk.

Access & systemic

Groin/radial access site, plus global vascular risk-factor control.

Anchor Surveillance has two arms running in parallel: imaging of the stented vessel and clinical neurologic assessment. Neither alone is enough.
What we watch for05
01 · In-Stent Restenosis

Definition & incidence

  • Mechanism differs from native disease. Early/intermediate ISR is neointimal hyperplasia (smooth-muscle proliferation), not lipid-rich plaque — smoother, more concentric, less embolic than de-novo lesions.
  • Two clinically used thresholds: ≥50% (moderate, watch) and ≥70–80% (severe, act).
  • Timing: highest hazard in the first 12–24 months; very late restenosis tends to be recurrent atherosclerosis.
  • Behaviour: a majority remain asymptomatic; stroke risk from ISR is lower than from primary plaque but not zero.
3–6%severe ISR (≥70%) at 1–2 years in contemporary series
<1–2%/yrISR-attributable ipsilateral stroke — lower than de-novo disease
Teaching point Reported ISR rates swing widely (≈3–30%) because the definition and the duplex criteria differ between studies. Always state which threshold you mean.

01 · Risk Stratification

Who restenoses? — risk factors for ISR

Patient / biology

Female sex · diabetes · active smoking · dyslipidaemia · chronic kidney disease · prior neck radiation (radiation-induced stenosis is high-risk).

Lesion / anatomy

Small reference diameter · long lesion · severe baseline calcification · recurrent (post-CEA) stenosis.

Procedure

Residual stenosis after stenting · stent under-expansion / malapposition · multiple overlapping stents.

Adherence

Antiplatelet non-compliance · uncontrolled risk factors · statin under-treatment.

Practical use High-risk patients (radiation, residual stenosis, post-CEA, diabetic) earn a tighter surveillance interval — not the same one-size schedule.

01 · Natural History

A timeline of risk — different problems, different windows

Early · 0–30 days

Procedural & haemodynamic

Acute/subacute stent thrombosis, embolic events, cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome, access-site complications, bradycardia/hypotension.

Intermediate · 1–24 mo

Neointimal hyperplasia

The dominant ISR mechanism. Smooth-muscle proliferation peaks — this is the window your duplex surveillance is built around.

Late · >2 years

Atherosclerosis & integrity

Recurrent atherosclerotic restenosis, contralateral progression, stent fracture / deformation. Justifies indefinite annual follow-up.

Mental model Match the modality and interval to the phase: dense early clinical vigilance → duplex through the hyperplasia window → lifelong annual checks.

02 · Modality of choice

Duplex ultrasound is the workhorse

  • Non-invasive, no contrast, no radiation — ideal for repeated, lifelong follow-up.
  • Gives both anatomy (B-mode/colour) and physiology (spectral Doppler velocities).
  • Detects the rising-velocity trend that flags developing ISR.
  • Cheap, bedside, repeatable — the basis of every guideline schedule.
But — one big caveat A stent stiffens the artery. Reduced wall compliance raises measured velocities even with a widely patent stent. Apply native-artery thresholds and you will over-call restenosis.
Operator-dependent Results hinge on a vascular lab that validates its own stented-carotid criteria and scans consistently.
Duplex ultrasound10
02 · The Pitfall

Why native-artery velocity thresholds mislead in a stent

  • Stent = a rigid tube. Loss of pulsatile wall expansion increases peak systolic velocity for the same lumen.
  • The effect is largest early after deployment and in long / stiff constructs.
  • Net result: a normal stented ICA often shows velocities that would mean “50% stenosis” in a native vessel.
  • The fix: use stent-specific, elevated cutoffs and weigh the serial trend over any single absolute number.
Native ICA (reference) ≥50%  →  PSV > 125 cm/s
≥70%  →  PSV > 230 cm/s
Stented ICA needs higher cutoffs → next slide Same degree of stenosis = a substantially higher PSV once a stent is in place.

Use the ratio

ICA/CCA ratio is less stiffness-dependent than absolute PSV — a robust tie-breaker.

Use the trend

A velocity climbing across serial scans beats any single value for calling early ISR.

Confirm before acting

02 · Interpretation

Baseline first, then trust the trend

  • Record a baseline PSV/EDV/ratio for each patient at ≤30 days — the stent's own “normal.”
  • Judge later scans against that baseline, not a population number.
  • A doubling of PSV or a clear upward trajectory is a red flag even below the absolute severe cutoff.
  • Keep the same lab and protocol where possible — inter-lab variability is real.

02 · Escalation

When duplex is not enough — confirmatory imaging

CT angiography

Strengths: fast, anatomic, arch & intracranial run-off.
Watch: stent blooming / artifact can overstate stenosis; needs contrast.

MR angiography

Strengths: no iodinated contrast / radiation.
Watch: susceptibility artifact from the stent; tends to overestimate.

Catheter DSA

Gold standard for quantifying ISR and planning re-intervention. Invasive — reserve for equivocal or treatment-bound cases.

Sequence Duplex screens → CTA/MRA confirms anatomy when duplex is equivocal or severe → DSA when you intend to treat.

03 · Foundation

Medical therapy underpins every surveillance plan

Antiplatelet therapy

DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel) for ~1 month post-CAS, then lifelong single agent — per device & local protocol.

High-intensity statin

Aggressive LDL lowering (± ezetimibe / PCSK9i). Plaque stabilisation lowers restenosis & stroke.

Risk-factor control

BP, glycaemic control, and smoking cessation — each independently drives ISR risk.

Adherence & education

Reinforce at every visit. Teach the patient the warning symptoms of TIA/stroke.

Frame Surveillance imaging detects trouble; optimised medical therapy is what prevents it. Do both.

Two worked examples — the watch pathway vs the act pathway

Case 1 · Worked example

Asymptomatic rising velocity at 12 months

  • 68 y/o man, right ICA CAS 12 months ago for a symptomatic 80% stenosis. Asymptomatic since; adherent to aspirin + high-intensity statin.
  • Serial duplex trend (not symptoms) is what triggered work-up — a steadily climbing PSV.
  • CTA: ~55% in-stent neointimal hyperplasia, smooth, no ulceration; stent well-apposed.
  • Decision: asymptomatic & <70% → intensify medical therapy (add ezetimibe, reinforce antiplatelet, tighten BP/glucose) and shorten interval to 6-monthly — not the cath lab.

Case 2 · Worked example

Symptomatic severe ISR in a high-risk neck

Summary

Five things to walk out with

  • Surveillance is non-negotiable — most in-stent restenosis is silent until it causes a stroke.
  • Duplex is the workhorse, on a baseline → 6 & 12 mo → annual schedule.
  • Use stent-specific velocity criteria (PSV ≥220 for ≥50%, ≥300–340 for ≥70%) — native thresholds over-call.
  • Trend > single number; a personal baseline makes every later scan interpretable.
  • Symptoms drive re-intervention; confirm with CTA/DSA, and never stop optimising medical therapy.
