CLINICAL CASE
Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement

Aortic Angulation
Feng Liang, MD,* Jiaxin Zhao, MD, PHD,* Yiming Zhang, MD, Chi Yuan, MD, Bingbing Zhang, MD, Jun Xie, MD, PHD
ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has become a ﬁrst-line treatment for elderly patients
with severe aortic stenosis since its ﬁrst successful implementation in 2002. However, performing TAVR in patients with
extreme horizontal heart orientation and acute aortic angulation is technically challenging and carries higher procedural
risks.
CASE SUMMARY This case report describes a patient with severe aortic stenosis complicated by an extreme horizontal
heart position and acute aortic angulation. Under the support of cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), femoral artery long
sheaths, and snare traction, the TAVR procedure was successfully completed.
DISCUSSION The success of this case provides valuable insights for managing complex TAVR procedures.
TAKE-HOME MESSAGES CPB provides critical hemodynamic support during TAVR in patients with extreme horizontal
cardiac orientation, effectively preventing intraoperative circulatory collapse. Advancing a femoral artery long sheath
combined with snare traction overcomes challenges when TAVR in cases of severe horizontal heart orientation and acute
aortic angulation (JACC Case Rep. 2025;30:104023) © 2025 Published by Elsevier on behalf of the American College of

INVESTIGATIONS
The result of a B-type natriuretic peptide test was 900
pg/mL. Electrocardiogram showed no signiﬁcant ab-
normalities.
Transthoracic
echocardiography
revealed a bicuspid aortic valve (BAV) with Sievers
type 1 morphology, severe aortic stenosis with mild-
to-moderate aortic regurgitation and mild tricuspid
regurgitation. Key measurements included an aortic
annulus diameter of 2.03 cm, peak transvalvular
pressure gradient of 76 mm Hg, and peak aortic valve
ﬂow velocity of 4.12 m/s. Subsequent aortic computed
tomography angiography evaluation demonstrated a
bicuspid aortic valve with an annular diameter of
22.4 mm and annular circumference of 70.5 mm. The
left ventricular outﬂow tract circumference measured
66.9 mm. Supra-annular measurements at 4mm/
6mm/8mm above the annulus revealed expandable
circumferences
of
62.6

respectively. Low coronary heights were noted. The
left coronary cusp length was 13.9 mm and right
was

mm.
Three-dimensional
reconstruction
conﬁrmed severe horizontal heart orientation, with
an aortic annular plane angle of 89 relative to the
horizontal plane in the coronal view, and an aortic
arch angulation of 79 (Figures 1A and 1B).
MANAGEMENT/INTERVENTION
The procedure was performed under general anes-
thesia. Following the induction of anesthesia, percu-
taneous access of the left femoral artery and vein was
ﬁrst achieved, and cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB)
was
successfully
established.
The
left
radial
artery
was
punctured
and
a
6-F
arterial
sheath
(RSA60K10SQ; Terumo) was placed, via which a 6-F
pigtail catheter (534-650S; Cordis) was positioned in
the aortic root. The right femoral artery and vein were
surgically exposed, followed by purse-string sutures
and placement of 6-F sheaths (RSA60K10SQ; Ter-
umo). A temporary pacing electrode (101771; St. Jude
Medical) was advanced via the right femoral venous
sheath to the right ventricular apex and connected to
an external pacemaker (Pace T10; Medtronic) as a
backup.
A
260-cm
J-tip
guidewire
(IQ35F260J3;
Merit
Medical) was advanced through the right femoral
artery sheath and guided to the aortic root. Over this
wire, another pigtail catheter (534-650S; Cordis) was
then positioned in the aortic root for exchanging a
Lunderquist guidewire (TSMG-35-260-LES, G31453;
Cook Medical). Over the Lunderquist guidewire, a 20-
F, 65-cm-long sheath (DSF2065; W.L. Gore & Associ-
ates) was successfully positioned in the ascending
aorta (Figure 2A).
A 6-F AL-2 catheter (534-645T; Cordis) was then
guided to the aortic root through the long sheath, and
a straight-tip wire (502-442; Cordis) was then navi-
gated through the stenotic aortic valve into the left
ventricle.
After
exchanging
for
the
260-cm
J-tip
guidewire, a pigtail catheter was placed in the left
ventricle, and a preshaped Lunderquist wire was
positioned at the ventricular apex to establish a stable
rail. A 20-mm balloon (VP-20040; MicroPort) was
advanced to the aortic valve and inﬂated during rapid
ventricular pacing (180 beats/min), with ﬂuoroscopy
conﬁrming adequate expansion (Figure 2B).
Subsequently, a 24-mm prosthetic valve (TAV24;
MicroPort) with an integrated delivery sheath was
advanced and adjusted to the deployed position with
the assistant of a 5-F MPA2 snare system (451-506H0;
Cordis), which was pretied to the delivery sheath
(Figure 2C). It should be emphasized that to ensure

DISCUSSION
Since Cribier et al1 ﬁrst reported TAVR in 2002, its
safety and efﬁcacy have been continuously validated.
TAVR is now established as a ﬁrst-line treatment for
high-risk, elderly patients with severe aortic stenosis
and has become a primary option for older adults with
aortic stenosis. However, as TAVR has expanded in
application, speciﬁc anatomical variations in certain
populations have been identiﬁed to increase proce-
dural difﬁculty and potentially impact success rates.
A classic challenge arises in cases of horizontal
aortic
root
orientation.
With
a
traditional
trans-
femoral
approach,
maintaining
coaxial
alignment
perpendicular to the aortic annular plane becomes
extremely difﬁcult, hindering successful crossing of
the annular plane. The "aortic angle" refers to the
angle between the aortic annular plane and the hor-
izontal plane in the coronal view. An aortic angle
>70 is deﬁned as an extreme horizontal aorta, in
which
the
heart
assumes
a
transverse
position,
creating severe misalignment between the aortic
annular plane and the delivery system.2 This com-
plicates
balloon
expansion
or
valve
deployment.
Recent advancements, such as the use of snare

To address these challenges, the multidisciplinary
valve team opted for CPB support to mitigate circu-
latory collapse. A femoral artery long sheath was
advanced across the aortic arch to enhance delivery
system stability and reduce vascular injury risk.
Finally, snare traction was applied to optimize coaxial
alignment during valve deployment. The transapical
TAVR approach effectively addresses challenges in
delivery

navigation
and
coaxial
valve
deployment.
However,
this
approach
was
not
selected in the present case due to its relatively
higher

and
longer
recovery
time
compared to alternative access routes. Techniques
and comparative advantages/disadvantages for TAVR
in patients with horizontal heart and severe aortic
arch angulation are listed in Table 1.
We selected a self-expanding valve (SEV) rather
than a balloon-expandable valve (BEV) in this case,
primarily due to the patient’s BAV morphology. Self-
expanding
valves
may
offer
reduced
procedural
risks and improved outcomes in such cases. The BEAT
(balloon versus self-expandable valve for the treat-
ment
of
bicuspid
aortic
valve
stenosis)
registry
demonstrated superior hemodynamic performance of
SEV in BEV cases, with signiﬁcantly lower mean
pressure gradients and lower annular rupture risk
compared
with
BEV.6
A
meta-analysis
also
corroborated these ﬁndings, concluding that SEV
achieves lower mean gradients and reduces annular
rupture
risk
in
BAV
patients.7
Furthermore,
the
MicroPort SEV
was
selected
because
its
second-
generation delivery system, VitaFlow Liberty, is the
world’s
only
commercially
available
electrically
retrievable delivery system, enabling intraoperative
adjustment or retrieval of the valve to ensure optimal
positioning, particularly in BAV patients of horizontal
aortic root orientation. Notably, the company’s third-
generation VitaFlow Liberty Flex steerable delivery
system is anticipated to further address challenges in
TAVR procedures for patients with horizontal heart
orientation or acute aortic arch angulation.
TAVR in patients with horizontal heart orientation
and severe aortic arch angulation remains technically
demanding. While the development of steerable de-
livery systems holds promise, current strategies face
limitations. This case demonstrates that combining
CPB support, femoral artery long sheaths, and snare
assistance signiﬁcantly improves procedural success
rates and reduces complications in such anatomically
complex
patients,
offering
valuable
insights
for
similar scenarios.
