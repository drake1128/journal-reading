ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND Vascular complications associated with transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) can prolong 
procedures, require additional interventions, extend hospital stays, delay recovery, and increase costs.
PROJECT RATIONALE Lake Charles Memorial Hospital identified a higher prevalence of peripheral artery disease 
in its TAVR population, compared with national benchmarks. This increased burden of complex vascular anatomy was 
initially associated with higher complication rates, prompting targeted prevention strategies to improve safety and 
outcomes.
PROJECT SUMMARY A multidisciplinary team used root-cause analyses and iterative plan-do-study-act cycles to 
integrate micropuncture, ultrasound guidance, fluoroscopic road mapping, closure strategies, and enhanced staff 
education into standardized workflows. From 2018 to the second quarter of 2025, complication rates decreased from 
11.7% to 1.9%, procedure times improved from 77 to 49 minutes, and hospital stays shortened, generating a net savings 
of over $86,000.

CASE SUMMARY PROMPTING THE 
PROJECT LAUNCH
This was a single-center, retrospective quality 
improvement (QI) project conducted at Lake Charles 
Memorial Hospital, a 313-bed community hospital in 
Southwest Louisiana. In 2019, Lake Charles Memorial 
Hospital became the first U.S. hospital to earn 
American College of Cardiology Transcatheter Valve 
Certification.
Baseline STS/ACC TVT Registry data (2018-2019) 
indicated that the hospital’s TAVR population had 
higher PAD prevalence compared with national 
benchmarks (47% vs 17%) (Table 1). 5 Vascular com-
plications averaged 11.7% (Figure 1), with 85% on the 
TAVR sheath access side, and often required surgical 
or endovascular repair (Figure 2). Given these find-
ings, a multidisciplinary QI initiative was launched in 
2020 to address vascular complications in patients
undergoing percutaneous transfemoral (TF) TAVR. 
Outcomes were evaluated across 3 phases: pre-
implementation (2018-2019), implementation (2020-
2022), and postimplementation (2023–second quarter 
[Q2] 2025).
PROJECT RATIONALE
In 2020, the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic com-
bined with multiple natural disasters, including 
Hurricanes Laura and Delta, a freeze, and flooding, 
amplified the urgency of reducing complications and 
conserving resources. Due to percutaneous TF access 
being the preferred route for TAVR, vascular safety 
was prioritized. A root-cause analysis using fishbone 
diagrams and pareto charts identified variations in 
access technique, limited advanced imaging and 
equipment, challenges with complex anatomy, 
closure device limitations, and inconsistent compli-
cation identification. These findings highlighted op-
portunities for shared learning and protocol change. 
Each complication added approximately $17,000 in 
total charges, $5,000 in direct costs, 27 minutes of 
procedural time, and 0.5 inpatient days.
Beyond lowering vascular complications, the QI 
initiative aimed to shorten procedure and recovery 
times, decrease patient length of stay, reduce costs, 
and improve patient outcomes during a time of 
extraordinary operational pressure.
PROJECT DESCRIPTION
The phased QI initiative was led by a multidisci-
plinary team including interventional cardiologists, a 
cardiovascular surgeon, imaging specialists, cardiac 
catheterization laboratory staff, and the valve clinic 
coordinator. Multidisciplinary reviews, supported by 
real-time STS/ACC TVT Registry metrics 5 and internal 
dashboards, enabled case review and complication 
tracking, and a prioritization matrix organized op-
portunities. Feedback from these sessions informed 
protocol refinements, consistent with iterative plan-
do-study-act (PDSA) cycles (Figure 3). Implementa-
tion phases with interventions are summarized in 
Figure 1, with detailed access workflow optimization 
shown in Figure 4.

IMPLEMENTATION PHASE (2020-2022)
Guided by root-cause analysis and iterative PDSA 
cycles, the QI team initiated changes to address 
identified gaps (Figure 3). In 2020, a large-bore 
closure device was selectively introduced for pa-
tients based on vessel size, calcification, and com-
mon femoral artery depth. 9 Preprocedure computed 
tomography angiography assessed iliofemoral anat-
omy, including vessel diameter, calcification, and 
tortuosity. In 2021, micropuncture kits were adopted 
to improve puncture precision at the point of 
maximal impact to optimize closure device deploy-
ment. 3 Acquisition of an ultrasound machine in 2022 
enabled routine ultrasound-guided access. Fluoro-
scopic road mapping was incorporated into the 
standardized protocol, overlaying the angiographic 
image onto live fluoroscopy to guide puncture at the 
point of maximal impact, with a micropuncture kit
for optimal closure, 2,7 as best results occur with 
minimal patient movement. Figure 4 outlines the 
sequential steps for optimizing percutaneous TF 
TAVR vascular access. Peripheral intravascular lith-
otripsy was added as an adjunct for heavily calcified 
iliofemoral stenosis, enhancing TF access and de-
livery safety. 10
Structured staff education for physicians, nurses, 
and imaging technicians emphasized groin assess-
ment and complication recognition within stan-
dardized protocols, reinforced through in-services, 
simulations, and competency validation.
POSTIMPLEMENTATION PHASE (2023-Q2 2025)
By the postimplementation phase, advanced imaging 
and access strategies were fully standardized and 
consistently applied. Real-time electronic medical 
record dashboards complemented STS/ACC TVT 
Registry benchmarking by enabling proactive moni-
toring and rapid response to emerging trends.

PROJECT DELIVERABLES
From 2018 through Q2 2025, the QI initiative 
delivered a structured process that combined new 
technologies with systematic process improve-
ments. Large-bore closure devices, micropuncture 
kits, ultrasound-guided access, fluoroscopic road 
mapping, and peripheral intravascular lithotripsy 
were introduced in a phased manner, aligned with 
iterative PDSA cycles (Figures 3 and 4). Each adop-
tion was accompanied by protocol development, 
staff training, and real-time performance moni-
toring to ensure consistent and safe integration into 
practice.
Regular multidisciplinary reviews and heart team 
meetings served as a forum for case discussion, 
complication tracking, and rapid feedback. Staff ed-
ucation for all team members was reinforced through 
competency validation, simulation, and in-service 
training. Together, these deliverables promoted 
consistency and reduced variability, embedding a 
culture of continuous QI across the catheterization 
laboratory.
PROJECT OUTCOME, IMPACT, AND 
FUTURE DIRECTIONS
From 2018 through Q2 2025, percutaneous TF access 
was used in 86% of TAVR cases (n = 350), with surgical 
TF cutdown and alternative access each accounting 
for 7%. Vascular complications declined from 11.7% 
(2018-2019) to 1.9% (2023-Q2 2025) (Figure 1), despite 
unchanged PAD prevalence (Table 1). Although the 
implementation phase included more low-risk pa-
tients, the postimplementation phase had the highest 
proportion of high-risk and inoperable patients, yet 
complication rates remained low, reflecting the posi-
tive effect of protocol standardization, team-based 
training, and ongoing performance review.
Among patients with a vascular complication 
within 30 days of TAVR (n = 23), 67% were female, 
with a mean age of 77 years and mean body mass 
index of 29.1 kg/m 2 . PAD was present in 42%, and 
mean STS risk score was 3.6%. Most patients (71%) 
were at intermediate surgical risk, and a 14-F TAVR 
delivery sheath was used in 96% of patients. The rate 
of major bleeding events related to vascular compli-
cations, as defined by VARC-2 criteria, 4 was 2.9% 
during preimplementation, 5.6% during imple-
mentation, and 0% during postimplementation; the 
rate of blood transfusions was 1.9%, 1.4%, and 0%, 
respectively.
Procedural efficiency improved from pre- to post-
implementation, with procedure time (stick-to-
closure) decreasing 36% (from 77 to 49 minutes), 
contrast volume reduced 55% (from 174 to 79 mL), 
fluoroscopy time lowered 55%, and average length of 
stay shortened from 2.0 to 1.25 days (Figure 5). 
Monitored anesthesia care increased from 74% pre-
implementation to 97% during implementation, and 
remained 92% postimplementation, with general 
anesthesia reserved for patients too unstable for 
monitored anesthesia care and/or alternate access 
cases (Table 1).

kits added approximately $201,823 in costs since 
2020, the savings from fewer complications should 
be $287,878 and the net savings should be $86,055
