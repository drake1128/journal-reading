ABSTRACT
Suicide left ventricle after transcatheter aortic valve replacement is a well described phenomenon associated with
increased morbidity and mortality. Prompt actions should be implemented to prevent this situation, and the alarm
signs should be recognized. We present a case report of successful recognition, prevention and treatment of this
complication. (Level of Difﬁculty: Intermediate.) (J Am Coll Cardiol Case Rep 2023;26:102065) © 2023 The Authors.

A 79-year-old man with severe aortic stenosis (AS)
was

for

(TAVR)

class III and he was hospitalized twice in the previous
6 months owing to acute pulmonary edema. His
physical examination revealed a systolic 5/6 heart
murmur at the second right intercostal space, and
there were no signs or symptoms of cardiac decom-
pensation. The risk scores were classifying him as low
risk (Euroscore II: 2.77%; STS score: 1.66%), but frailty
(Clinical Frailty Scale: 6) as well as the patient’s and
the family’s preference favored TAVR instead of sur-
gery. His electrocardiogram was in sinus rhythm with
ﬁrst-degree atrioventricular block and right bundle
branch block.
In the baseline echocardiographic study concen-
tric left ventricular (LV) hypertrophy was evident
with septal bulge appearance (end-diastolic septal
thickness: 16 mm) and normal LV systolic function
(LV
ejection
fraction:
60%).

MANAGEMENT
Dynamic intraventricular gradient (DIG) has been
described as a factor of acute cardiac failure after
TAVR with possibly unpredictable evolution.2 We
aimed
to
prevent
the
post-TAVR
hemodynamic
sequela of “suicide left ventricle” phenomenon by
decreasing
the
LVOT
gradient
and
planning
the
therapeutic steps after TAVR if the prevention was
not successful. Therefore, we implemented measures
to increase the LV preload (nebivolol continuation
and furosemide interruption) and planned the TAVR
5 days after. The day of the TAVR procedure, the
patient presented with mild bilateral pitting leg
edemas, but no dyspnea was evident. The pre-TAVR
echocardiography
demonstrated
decrease
of
the
LVOT peak gradient to 34 mm Hg (Figure 2). Despite
the volume overload, the patient received intrave-
nous (IV) saline solution at 100 mL/h during the
procedure. After gaining arterial access, we pro-
ceeded with the hemodynamic measurements. The
LVapex-Aorta
mean
gradient
was
calculated
at
66 mm Hg, reﬂecting both LVOT and AV gradients,
whereas after placing a temporary right ventricular
(RV) pacing electrode and paced by overdriving the
patient’s rate, the mean gradient was reduced to
51 mm Hg. That was a valuable information, implying
that RV pacing–induced desynchrony was effective in
lowering the DIG (Figure 3). An Evolut Pro 29 mm
valve (Medtronic) was delivered and placed success-
fully with no balloon predilation. After the valve
expansion, third-degree atrioventricular block was
detected. LVapex-Aorta mean gradient under pacing
after TAVR was 20 mm Hg, highlighting the LVOT
gradient (Figure 3).
Rapidly after the procedure, the patient became
hypotensive with blood pressure 75/60 mm Hg. No
hemorrhagic cause was detected and rapid bedside
echocardiography showed hypercontractility of the
LV, normal function of the prosthetic valve and no
pericardial effusion (Video 1). Rapid IV saline loading

DISCUSSION
Altering
cardiac
hemodynamics
after
TAVR
may
hide surprises in speciﬁc populations. In the present
case, we showed that the “suicide left ventricle”
phenomenon can be prevented or at least alleviated
by preventive measures and targeted simple thera-
peutic actions. Treatment should aim to increase
preload and afterload to support LV ﬁlling pressures
and mitigate LV hypercontractility.
Dynamic
LVOT
obstruction
is
characteristically
encountered in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, but is
well described in chronic pressure overload such as in
AS. The LV adapts to the chronic increased afterload

FOLLOW-UP
The
1-month

The

CONCLUSIONS
The clinical phenomenon of “suicide left ventricle”
after TAVR is associated with worse clinical outcomes
and may be anticipated and successfully prevented
with implementation of simple measures. Targeting
the increase of LV preload and afterload is the key,
and if prevention is not enough, speciﬁc therapeutic
strategies may provide hemodynamic support and
ameliorate the circulatory compromise.
