ABSTRACT
Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) and percutaneous balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty (PBPV) are the ﬁrst-line
treatments for aortic stenosis and pulmonary stenosis. This paper presents the case of a 72-year-old woman who suffered
from severe aortic stenosis with pulmonary stenosis, treated with simultaneous TAVR and PBPV. Postprocedural echo-
cardiography revealed signiﬁcant improvement in valve stenosis, with no complications. This case demonstrates the
feasibility and safety of performing TAVR and PBPV together, offering a new treatment approach. (JACC Case Rep.

A
72-year-old woman with a history of chronic
obstructive
pulmonary
disease
requiring
home oxygen therapy was admitted to the
hospital due to dyspnea. On physical examination,
her vital signs included a pulse of 94 beats/min, a
respiration rate of 26 breaths/min, and a blood pres-
sure of 155/70 mm Hg. Cyanosis was noted on her
lips. Cardiac examination revealed a regular heart
rhythm with a 3/6 systolic murmur over the aortic
and pulmonary valves, and 2þ edema in the lower
extremities.
Laboratory tests showed an N-terminal pro–B-type
natriuretic peptide of 1895.3 pg/mL. Echocardiogra-
phy indicated myocardial ischemia in the inferior wall
(Figure
1).
Transthoracic
echocardiography
(TTE)
revealed severe aortic stenosis (AS), with a jet veloc-
ity of 4.87 m/s and a mean gradient of 57 mm Hg
(1 mm Hg ¼ 0.133 kPa) (Figure 2D).
For the pulmonary valve, TTE showed thickened
leaﬂets, high-speed turbulent ﬂow during systole,
and Doppler hemodynamics conﬁrming severe pul-
monary stenosis (PS) with a jet velocity of 5.46 m/s
and a gradient of 119 mm Hg (Figures 2A to 2C).
Additional ﬁndings included ventricular hypertrophy
(13 mm interventricular septum and 12 mm left
ventricular posterior wall), pulmonary trunk dilation
(39 mm), and a left ventricular ejection fraction
of 72%.
The primary diagnoses were severe AS, severe PS,
and diastolic heart failure (NYHA functional class III),
categorized as heart failure with preserved ejection
fraction.

DEVICE AND PROCEDURE
The TaurusElite (Peijia Medical) is a self-expandable
heart valve system composed of a nickel-titanium
frame, a trileaﬂet bovine pericardial valve, and a
double-deck skirt, both inside and outside the frame.
The speciﬁcations and models of the prosthetic valve
are shown in Figure 6. Given that the supra-annular
valve design and double-deck skirt help optimize
postoperative
hemodynamics
and
reduce
peri-
valvular leakage, we selected the TaurusElite for this
procedure.
First, a straight guidewire was passed across the

to establish the TAVR pathway. Left heart catheteri-
zation

severe

113 mm Hg. Next, another 6-F pigtail catheter was

perform

which

22-  40-mm TaurusAtlas balloon (Peijia Medical) was

for predilation (Figure 7B). The TaurusElite AV26

delivered and released, with the gradient decreasing
to 5 mm Hg (Figure 7C). Finally, a 25-  40-mm

isolated AS.2 A signiﬁcant proportion of patients

PS is a prevalent congenital heart disease, with an
incidence rate of approximately 8% to 10% among all
congenital
heart
diseases,4
whereas
in
older
in-
dividuals with valvular heart disease in China, the
rate is <1%.2 PBPV is a preferred treatment for
patients of all age groups.5 The coexistence of AS
and PS is rare, and no relevant data are available.
Gustafson et al6 reported a case of a patient who
underwent sequential TAVR and transcatheter pul-
monary valve replacement for symptomatic AS and
PS in bioprosthetic valve dysfunction, resulting in a
favorable outcome. However, there are no additional
clinical studies or case reports on the combined use of
TAVR and PBPV.
The patient in this case presented with severe
symptomatic AS and PS of the native valves. The
etiologies of AS and PS differ signiﬁcantly. Although
Williams syndrome can cause both AS and PS, this
patient did not show any typical features of the syn-
drome (eg, limb abnormalities, intellectual disability,
coagulation
issues,
atrioventricular
structural
abnormalities). Additionally, there was no family
history of similar conditions. Given the patient’s age,
clinical presentation, and calciﬁcation of both aortic
and pulmonary valves, the aortic valve pathology was
considered
degenerative,
whereas
the
pulmonary
valve was suspected to be a congenital dome-shaped
valve.
The challenge in this case was determining the
order of interventions for simultaneous TAVR and
PBPV. Treating AS ﬁrst could result in insufﬁcient
blood ﬂow into the left ventricle, leading to severe
peripheral hypotension and even death, particularly
given this patient’s suicidal left ventricle, which
could cause near-complete occlusion and signiﬁcant
drops in cardiac output.7 On the other hand, treating
PS ﬁrst could lead to severe pulmonary edema.
To reduce these risks, we chose to perform PBPV
before TAVR. Rather than a full dilation of the pul-
monary valve in one step, we opted for a gradual
approach. This strategy allowed for partial relief of
PS, enabling improved blood ﬂow to the left side of

outcome in this case, the combination of PBPV and
TAVR appears feasible. The step-by-step approach
helped manage hemodynamic changes and reduced
the risk of hemodynamic collapse.
However, several limitations exist in this proced-
ure. First, we did not adequately monitor the pres-

prevented
us
from
observing

the pulmonary valve, indicating that PS may not be
fully resolved by PBPV. Although the patient’s he-
modynamics were stable 30 days after the procedure,
annual ultrasound monitoring is still required due to
the residual stenosis. If the stenosis progresses, then
surgical pulmonary valve replacement may need to be
considered for this patient.

CONCLUSIONS
This case demonstrates that simultaneous TAVR and
PBPV in a patient with AS and PS can be performed
safely and feasibly. A strategic approach to the pro-
cedures helps reduce the risk of hemodynamic com-
plications. However, this combination of valvular
diseases is rare, and more research is needed to better
understand the etiology and mechanisms involved.
Similarly, procedural strategies for combining TAVR
with PBPV require further exploration.
