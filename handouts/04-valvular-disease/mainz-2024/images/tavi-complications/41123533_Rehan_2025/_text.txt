VALVULAR HEART DISEASE
CLINICAL CASE
Intravascular Lithotripsy-Enabled 
Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement 
in Heavily Calcified Bicuspid Aortic Valve
Rajan Rehan, MBBS, a Alexandra Moss, MBCHB, b James Cotton, MD, b Benjamin Wrigley, MD, b Shazad Munir, MD, b 
Saib Khogali, MD a,b
ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) in bicuspid aortic valves (BAVs), especially with severe 
calcification, presents challenges including prosthesis underexpansion and paravalvular leak.
CASE SUMMARY We present a critically unwell 70-year-old man with severe aortic stenosis in a heavily calcified 
Sievers-0 BAV, complicated by heart failure and chronic kidney disease. Intravascular lithotripsy (IVL) was used for 
calcium modification, followed by balloon valvuloplasty and deployment of a 26-mm Evolut PRO+ valve. The patient 
had excellent clinical recovery, with no paravalvular leak and improved ventricular function.
DISCUSSION This case highlights the novel use of IVL to facilitate TAVR in a complex calcified BAV anatomy. IVL 
enables controlled calcium fracture and improves compliance, optimizing prosthesis expansion and reducing compli-
cations. However, the lack of dedicated valvular IVL platforms remains a current limitation.

MANAGEMENT
After multidisciplinary heart team discussion, a de-
cision was made to proceed with aortic valve calcium 
modification using intravascular lithotripsy (IVL), 
with cerebral embolic protection (CEP), to optimize 
prosthesis expansion and minimize paravalvular 
leak. The procedure was performed under local 
anesthesia and conscious sedation. Ultrasound-
guided bifemoral arterial access was obtained, with 
a 6-F sheath inserted in the right femoral artery (RFA) 
for valve deployment and a 6-F sheath in the left 
femoral artery for angiographic guidance. A 7-F 
sheath was placed in the left femoral vein for intra-
venous access and temporary pacing. Preclosure of 
the RFA was performed using a single Perclose 
ProGlide (Abbott) device, followed by advancement 
of a 9-F sheath. A 6-F sheath was also inserted in the 
right radial artery for cerebral protection. The 
Sentinel CEP device (Boston Scientific) was success-
fully deployed, with both baskets remaining in situ 
throughout the procedure. A 5-F balloon-tipped 
temporary pacing wire was then advanced into the 
right ventricle.
The BAV was crossed using a 0.035-inch wire, 
which was subsequently exchanged for a 300-cm 
balance middleweight wire via a 5-F pigtail cath-
eter. A JR4 guide catheter was then used to facilitate 
the delivery of a 7-mm Shockwave balloon.
A 7 mm × 60 mm peripheral Shockwave balloon was 
advanced over the balance middleweight wire, suc-
cessfully crossing the BAV, where it became con-
strained within the 4.4-mm slit-like valve aperture 
(Figure 3). IVL was performed with sequential balloon 
inflations; the balloon remained constrained within 
the valve, eliminating the need for rapid pacing. The 
7-mm Shockwave balloon was inflated to 4 atm, with 
90 IVL pulses delivered before full expansion was 
achieved (Figure 4, Video 1). After IVL, the balloon 
was cautiously upsized to 22 mm and subsequently to 
24 mm for balloon aortic valvuloplasty, both per-
formed under rapid pacing (Figure 5, Video 2).
A 29-mm Evolut PRO+ valve was initially selected 
based on annular dimensions and measurements 
taken 4 mm above the annulus, with a low threshold 
for downsizing to accommodate the Sievers-0 BAV 
morphology. However, the valve remained exces-
sively constrained with infolding, prompting a deci-
sion to downsize to a 26-mm Evolut PRO+ valve 
(Figure 6, Video 3). The valve was deployed in an 
optimally high position and successfully released. 
Gradual postdilatation was performed under rapid 
pacing, initially using a 22-mm balloon and subse-
quently upsizing to a 24-mm balloon (Figure 7, 
Video 4). This resulted in a well-expanded valve with 
excellent hemodynamic performance, no para-
valvular leak, and a mean transvalvular gradient of 
6 mm Hg on postprocedural echocardiography. Left 
ventricular systolic function demonstrated immedi-
ate improvement, increasing from 15% preprocedure 
to an estimated 35% postprocedure (Figure 8,

OUTCOME AND FOLLOW-UP
The patient had excellent recovery on the ward, with 
significant postprocedural diuresis. He demonstrated 
a marked clinical improvement after TAVR and was 
discharged home after the procedure on day 4, with 
planned follow-up at 6 to 8 weeks. A repeat 
computed tomography TAVR was performed to 
assess the prosthesis, confirming satisfactory valve 
expansion and apposition, with an ellipsoid config-
uration (Figure 9).
DISCUSSION
TAVR has now replaced surgical aortic valve 
replacement as the predominant modality for aortic 
valve replacement, with expanding indications 
including patients with BAVs. 1,2 A heavily calcified 
anatomy poses significant technical challenges, 
particularly in high-risk patients with multiple 
comorbidities. These cases are associated with an 
increased risk of suboptimal valve expansion, para-
valvular leak, and annular rupture. 3 Such complex-
ities may necessitate incorporation of advanced 
calcium modification strategies to optimize lesion 
preparation and facilitate safe and effective valve 
deployment.
IVL, an established therapy for calcium modifica-
tion in coronary and peripheral artery disease, uses 
sonic pressure waves to induce multiplanar calcium 
fractures, enhancing lesion compliance. 4 This tech-
nology is now emerging as a valuable adjunct in 
structural heart interventions, with promising po-
tential in facilitating transcatheter mitral and pul-
monic valve procedures. 5-7 Recently, there have been 
isolated reports of IVL facilitating aortic valve 
balloon valvuloplasty and TAVR. 8-11 Because severe 
calcification remains a major challenge in TAVR, IVL 
may offer a novel approach to improve valve 
compliance, optimizing prosthesis expansion and 
reducing complications. 12
Our case highlights a novel stepwise approach 
using IVL to modify severe calcification in a complex 
Sievers-0 BAV, allowing successful TAVR in a patient
with 
critical 
decompensation. 
IVL 
facilitated 
controlled calcium disruption, enabling further pre-
dilatation with 22-mm and 24-mm valvuloplasty 
balloons to minimize geometric constraints on pros-
thesis deployment. Adjunctive use of the Sentinel 
CEP device mitigated the risk of cerebrovascular 
events from particulate embolization during IVL of a 
heavily calcified valve.
Despite its potential, aortic valve IVL remains 
constrained by the lack of appropriately sized 
balloons for comprehensive annular and leaflet 
modification. The development of dedicated, larger-
diameter 
platforms 
specifically 
designed 
for 
valvular applications remains an unmet clinical need. 
Next-generation adjuvant technologies combining 
enhanced 
calcium-modifying 
capabilities 
with 
improved deliverability may provide more predict-
able valve expansion while mitigating risks of pros-
thesis underexpansion or malapposition. Given the 
unique challenges posed by severely calcified anato-
mies, further research is warranted to define optimal 
lesion preparation strategies and establish standard-
ized procedural algorithms for this high-risk cohort.
CONCLUSIONS
We describe the novel and successful use of IVL to 
optimize lesion preparation in a severely calcified 
Sievers-0 BAV, facilitating successful TAVR. This 
case highlights IVL’s potential to facilitate TAVR, 
although the lack of dedicated balloon platforms for 
valvular applications remains a limitation. Because 
TAVR expands to more complex anatomies, ad-
vancements in calcium modification technologies are 
needed to improve procedural outcomes. Further 
research is essential to define optimal strategies and 
establish IVL’s role in aortic valve interventions.
