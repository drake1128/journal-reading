CLINICAL CASE
Surgical Findings of Iatrogenic
Aortic Regurgitation After Percutaneous 
Left Atrial Appendage Device Retrieval
Hiroto Kamitani, MD, a Hiroshi Nakanaga, MD, a Minoru Tabata, MD, PHD, MPH a,b
ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND Device dislodgment during percutaneous left atrial appendage occlusion is a rare but challenging 
complication. Although both catheter-based and surgical retrieval have been described, the optimal strategy remains 
unclear.
CASE SUMMARY A 57-year-old man developed severe aortic regurgitation after transcatheter retrieval of a left atrial 
appendage occlusion device that had embolized into the left ventricle. He subsequently underwent minimally invasive 
aortic valve replacement.
DISCUSSION Transcatheter retrieval of a dislodged device from the left ventricle poses significant risk. Manipulation 
across the aortic valve may cause valvular injury, as in this case. Early surgical consultation is essential, because surgical 
retrieval provides direct visualization and controlled management of cardiac structures, with the option for concomitant 
left atrial appendage occlusion.
TAKE-HOME MESSAGE When device embolization into the left ventricle occurs, early surgical evaluation is advised 
to minimize valve injury and ensure safe management. (JACC Case Rep. 2026;31:107381) © 2026 The Authors.

HISTORY OF PRESENTATION
A 57-year-old man underwent percutaneous left 
atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) at another insti-
tution. However, echocardiography performed after 
the patient left the catheterization laboratory 
revealed device embolization into the left ventricle. 
Then, the patient was brought back to the catheteri-
zation laboratory. Transaortic retrieval was per-
formed via catheter-based manipulation across the 
aortic valve, using an 18-F sheath advanced into the 
left ventricle and a snare catheter to capture and 
retrieve the device. This was followed by successful 
reimplantation of a new device of larger size than the 
initial one. During the retrieval process, the patient 
developed severe iatrogenic aortic regurgitation

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY
The patient had a history of paroxysmal atrial fibril-
lation, transient ischemic attack, and premature 
ventricular contractions.
DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
No differential diagnoses were considered because 
the etiology of the AR was clearly attributable to the 
catheter-based retrieval procedure.
INVESTIGATIONS
Electrocardiography revealed normal sinus rhythm. 
Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated se-
vere AR caused by prolapse of the right coronary 
cusp. The left ventricular end-diastolic and end-
systolic diameters were 56 and 34 mm, respectively, 
with a preserved left ventricular ejection fraction of 
66%. No mitral stenosis or regurgitation was 
observed. 
Transesophageal 
echocardiography 
revealed a tear of the right coronary cusp near the 
right and noncoronary commissure, resulting in se-
vere AR (Figures 1A and 1B). It also revealed an iat-
rogenic atrial septal defect (ASD) resulting from the 
Brockenbrough technique (Figure 1C) and a coexisting 
patent foramen ovale (PFO). The newly implanted 
LAAO device was visualized in the correct position 
within the left atrial appendage, showing minimal 
overlap with the mitral valve (Figure 1D). Computed 
tomography showed an area-derived aortic annulus 
diameter of 24.6 mm. The diameters of the sinus of 
Valsalva were 37.4 mm (right coronary sinus),
35.7 mm (left coronary sinus), and 36.5 mm (non-
coronary sinus). Coronary heights were 14.5 mm for 
the right coronary artery and 12.1 mm for the left 
main trunk. There was no significant coronary artery 
stenosis or malalignment.
MANAGEMENT (MEDICAL/INTERVENTIONS)
The patient underwent minimally invasive aortic 
valve replacement along with closure of 2 ASDs and a 
PFO via an endoscopic right mini-thoracotomy 
approach (Video 1). After aortic cross-clamping and 
aortotomy, endoscopically guided selective ante-
grade cardioplegia was administered, 1 achieving 
effective myocardial protection and prompt cardiac 
arrest. Two iatrogenic ASDs and a PFO were identi-
fied and closed directly through a left atriotomy 
(Figure 2A). The large LAAO device was positioned in 
the left atrial appendage such that it partially over-
laid the mitral valve (Figure 2B). Approximately one-
half of the right coronary cusp was torn near the 
leaflet insertion (Figure 2C), and a durable repair was 
deemed infeasible. In addition, multiple focal endo-
cardial abrasions were identified on the left ventric-
ular septal wall, which were considered to be caused 
by contact during the prior device retrieval procedure 
(Figure 2D). The valve leaflets were excised, and a 
25-mm bovine pericardial prosthesis was implanted

OUTCOME AND FOLLOW-UP
The patient had an uneventful postoperative course 
and was discharged on postoperative day 5. Predis-
charge echocardiography showed no AR or intracar-

DISCUSSION
Device dislodgment during percutaneous LAAO has 
been reported to occur in 0.6% to 3.9% of cases. 2,3 In 
a review of 108 reported cases, the left ventricle was 
identified as the second most common site of 
embolization, accounting for 36 cases (33.3%), and 
was associated with a higher mortality rate. 4 To our 
knowledge, this is the first reported case of aortic 
valve injury caused by transaortic retrieval of a dis-
lodged 
LAAO 
device. 
As 
this 
case 
clearly
demonstrates, such retrieval can result in significant 
aortic valve damage, potentially leading to severe AR 
and serious clinical consequences. Extreme caution 
is therefore warranted when considering this 
approach. Although catheter-based retrieval tech-
niques—including transseptal approaches—have been 
successfully performed in selected cases, 5 they carry 
significant risks. Notably, fatal mitral valve injury has 
been documented, 6 underscoring the potential haz-
ards of percutaneous retrieval in certain anatomic 
settings. When surgical risk is acceptable, minimally 
invasive surgical retrieval using cardiopulmonary 
bypass via an endoscopic right mini-thoracotomy 
and left atriotomy offers several distinct advan-
tages. This strategy provides clear endoscopic visu-
alization, reduces the risk of intracardiac structural 
injury, and enables concomitant surgical procedures 
such as iatrogenic ASD closure, surgical LAAO, and 
atrial fibrillation surgery, when necessary. In this 
case, according to the available information, trans-
catheter retrieval was performed without surgical 
consultation. When a large device such as an LAAO 
occluder embolizes into the left ventricle, prompt 
consultation with cardiac surgeons is advisable so 
that the heart team can jointly determine the safest

occurs, a second transcatheter LAAO often necessi-

CONCLUSIONS
We report a case in which minimally invasive aortic 
valve replacement was successfully performed to 
treat severe AR caused by transcatheter retrieval of a
dislodged LAAO device. This case highlights that 
transcatheter retrieval, particularly when the device 
has migrated into the left ventricle, carries a sub-
stantial risk of valvular injury and should therefore 
be approached with great caution. In such circum-
stances, early surgical consultation and consider-
ation of surgical retrieval may provide a safer and 
more controlled management strategy.
