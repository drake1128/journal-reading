ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND Transfemoral access is the preferred route for transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), but 
severe calcification and luminal narrowing of the iliofemoral artery or abdominal aorta may preclude safe large-bore 
sheath passage. Intravascular lithotripsy (IVL) has been increasingly used for iliofemoral vessel preparation, yet its 
application in the abdominal aorta has been rarely reported.
CASE SUMMARY An 84-year-old woman with symptomatic severe aortic stenosis was deemed high risk for 
transfemoral TAVR owing to severe narrowing with circumferential calcification of infrarenal aorta with luminal 
diameter <5 mm. To preserve transfemoral access, IVL with a 7.0-mm Shockwave balloon was performed using stepwise 
low-pressure inflations (1-6 atm, 300 pulses), achieving adequate luminal expansion for an 18-F sheath. A 23-mm 
self-expanding valve was successfully implanted without complications, and the patient was discharged the next day.
DISCUSSION This case demonstrates that IVL can safely modify a small-caliber, circumferentially calcified 
iliofemoral and abdominal aorta to facilitate transfemoral TAVR.
TAKE-HOME MESSAGE Stepwise low-pressure inflations and meticulous procedural planning may expand
the feasibility of transfemoral access in patients otherwise considered for higher-risk alternative routes.

HISTORY OF PRESENTATION
An 84-year-old woman with symptomatic severe 
aortic stenosis was referred for TAVR evaluation. 
Over the preceding year, she developed progressive 
fatigue and occasional lightheadedness. Her exercise 
tolerance declined, with increased need for rest 
during daily activities, though she was still able to 
continue short daily walks.

INVESTIGATIONS
Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated se-
vere aortic stenosis with a mean gradient of 
42 mm Hg and valve area of 0.7 cm 2 . Multidetector 
computed tomography (CT) confirmed a small native 
aortic annulus with an area of 274 mm 2 , consistent 
with a small annular anatomy. CT angiography 
revealed circumferential heavy calcification of the 
infrarenal abdominal aorta with a minimal luminal 
diameter of 4.9 × 5.7 mm (mean 5.3 mm) and an 
external diameter of <10 mm (mean 9.1 mm), 
consistent with calcified abdominal aorta stenosis 
with negative remodeling. Bilateral common iliac
arteries were also severely narrowed (right 5.3 mm 
mean, left 4.7 mm mean) with eccentric calcification 
(Figure 1). These findings identified the abdominal 
aorta and iliac arteries as the principal barriers to 
transfemoral access.
MANAGEMENT STRATEGY
To preserve transfemoral access, IVL of the infrarenal 
aorta and right iliac artery was planned using an 
undersized balloon and stepwise low-pressure in-
flations. If unsuccessful, alternative access was pre-
pared as a bailout strategy.

residual transvalvular gradient, and absence of par-

complications (Figure 2B). Hemostasis was secured 
using an 18-F MANTA closure device (Teleflex) at 
the femoral access site and a radial compression 
device at the radial access site. The patient tolerated 
the procedure well and was discharged the 
following day in stable condition.
DISCUSSION
Severe calcification of the iliofemoral or abdominal 
aorta is a recognized challenge for transfemoral TAVR, 
often precluding large-bore sheath passage and 
necessitating alternative access strategies such as 
transaxillary, transcaval, or transcarotid approaches. 
Although these alternatives are feasible, they are 
associated with higher complication rates and less 
favorable outcomes compared with transfemoral ac-
cess. Preserving the transfemoral route is therefore of 
great clinical importance whenever possible.
IVL has recently emerged as a valuable adjunctive 
tool to facilitate transfemoral TAVR in patients with

acoustic shockwaves through a low-pressure balloon, 
IVL produces microfractures in calcium while mini-
mizing barotrauma to soft tissue. This mechanism 
improves vessel compliance without the rupture risk 
associated with conventional high-pressure angio-
plasty. Multiple registries and case series have 
demonstrated the feasibility and safety of IVL for 
iliofemoral access preparation. 1
In contrast, the use of IVL in the abdominal aorta 
has been infrequently reported, with only a few case 
reports describing its application in settings such as 
coral reef aorta. 2,3 The unique aspect of our case is 
the presence of circumferential atherosclerotic 
calcification with negative remodeling of the 
infrarenal abdominal aorta, characterized by an 
external diameter of only approximately 9 mm and a 
lumen of approximately 5 mm. This small-caliber 
phenotype, resulting from constrictive vessel 
remodeling, contrasts with previously reported 
cases. It highlights a distinct pattern of advanced 
atherosclerosis that may be particularly relevant in 
elderly patients with multiple comorbidities. 4 
In this challenging anatomy, IVL permitted 
controlled vessel expansion at low balloon pressures, 
reducing rupture risk compared with high-pressure 
angioplasty and enabling safe transfemoral TAVR. 
Several technical considerations are noteworthy: 1) 
careful balloon sizing, with a 7.0-mm balloon 
selected to match the narrowed lumen and avoid 
oversizing; 2) gradual, stepwise low-pressure in-
flations to minimize vessel injury; and 3) compre-
hensive bailout planning, including availability of 
covered stents and surgical backup. These pre-
cautions are critical when applying IVL to large cen-
tral vessels such as the abdominal aorta, where 
complications could be catastrophic.

In this case of native aortic stenosis with extensive 
aortoiliac calcification, a self-expanding Evolut 
FX + valve was selected owing to its flexible 
over-the-wire delivery catheter and tapered distal 
profile, which facilitated smoother advancement 
through the IVL-treated aortic segment. Preproce-
dural CT demonstrated a small annular area, consis-
tent with a small native aortic annulus. This anatomy 
further supported the use of a self-expanding, supra-
annular valve design, which provides a larger effec-
tive orifice area and lower residual gradients 
compared with intra-annular balloon-expandable 
valves in small annuli. Although the Evolut system is 
typically advanced without an introducer sheath, an 
18-F Gore DrySeal sheath was intentionally used in 
this case to provide additional hemostasis and pro-
cedural stability and to protect the IVL-modified 
aortic segment during catheter exchanges. 
Clinically, this case demonstrates that IVL can 
extend the feasibility of transfemoral TAVR in 
anatomically prohibitive patients. Recent data indi-
cate that transfemoral TAVR with adjunctive pe-
ripheral intervention is associated with more 
favorable outcomes compared with nontransfemoral 
approaches, highlighting the importance of preser-
ving transfemoral access whenever possible. 5 
Although early results are encouraging, systematic
data are lacking. Further studies are needed to define 
the safety, durability, and long-term outcomes of 
abdominal aortic IVL in this setting.
CONCLUSIONS
This case demonstrates that IVL of the abdominal 
aorta can be performed safely and effectively to 
facilitate transfemoral TAVR in patients with pro-
hibitive calcification and small-caliber anatomy. Us-
ing an undersized balloon with gradual low-pressure 
inflations allowed controlled vessel expansion, 
enabling large-bore sheath delivery without vascular 
injury. Careful patient selection, meticulous plan-
ning, and preparedness for bailout remain essential 
when applying IVL to central vessels, where compli-
cations could be catastrophic. Although this single 
case illustrates feasibility, further experience and 
systematic data are needed to define the role of 
abdominal aortic IVL in expanding transfemoral ac-
cess options.
